# Candidates — The Meeting That Should Have Been One Line

## Soal (read first)

Write ONE line: the single sentence that could have replaced an entire pointless
meeting. No context, no explanation, no setup. Gate: `/output/entry.txt` must be
UTF-8 text of 1 to 200 characters, not only whitespace. Prize: $2, WINNER-TAKE-ALL
(1st only). 1,275 subs already. Deadline Nov 26 20:30 WIB; round 1 cutoff Nov 26
04:29 WIB. Judge: house judge (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash), head-to-head pairs both orders,
3 Swiss rounds + playoff among top 3.

## Judge brief (competition.yaml, verbatim order of importance)

Persona: a deadpan office satirist who has survived more pointless meetings than
anyone should. Weigh, most important first:

1. Is a single line, one sentence that could have replaced an entire pointless
   meeting, unmistakably office humor — and loses to any one-liner that kills a
   pointless meeting.
2. Kills the meeting outright: after this line, no one could justify holding the
   meeting at all.
3. Feels relatable to anyone who has sat through a useless meeting, drawn from
   real office life.
4. Lands as a joke, with a turn or a sting that makes it funny rather than
   merely true.
5. Stands alone as a single line, needing no context or explanation.

Length for its own sake does not count. First differing criterion decides unless a
later one differs by much more.

## Method

40 candidates, scored 1–5 on the judge's criteria in the judge's order:

- **A — Kills the meeting outright** (criterion 2): the line itself contains the
  decision / outcome / news, so no meeting can be justified after it. A line that
  only comments on meetings, or only cancels one without replacing its content,
  scores low here.
- **B — Relatable** (criterion 3): drawn from real office life anyone recognises.
- **C — Funny turn / sting** (criterion 4): surprise, deadpan twist, punch — not
  merely true.
- **D — One sentence, stands alone, unmistakably office humor** (criteria 1 & 5).
- **E — Freshness / distinctness**: not the cliché every entrant writes, and not
  a near-copy of a visible competitor line or a likely house anchor text (an entry
  reproducing a house text takes no prize). "Could have been an email" and
  "per my last email" were excluded outright as the most duplicated lines in the
  field and the most likely house texts.

Total /25. Gate: 1–200 chars, exactly one sentence, no trailing newline.

## Blind head-to-head tournament (top 8, round-robin, 28 pairs)

Each pair judged blind by the brief's criteria in order: first differing
criterion decides unless a later one differs by much more. Verdicts:

- #29 beats #4, #15, #30, #3, #11, #12, #14 (7–0): the only line that IS the
  whole meeting — it delivers real news (delay + client knows + unchangeable),
  so it replaces a meeting that actually had content, not just an empty one.
- #15 beats #4, #30, #3, #11, #12, #14; loses to #29 (6–1): the canonical
  no-update status meeting, and "this same sentence in two weeks" is the
  second-best sting — but it replaces a meeting with no content, an easier kill.
- #4 beats #30, #3, #11, #12, #14; loses to #29, #15 (5–2): "already done it,
  and it took four minutes" is the sharpest single sting, but its scope is one
  task, not a whole meeting.
- #30 beats #3, #11, #12, #14; loses to #29, #15, #4 (4–3): the legal
  deadlock is circular and funny, but a stalemate is not a resolution.
- #14 beats #3, #11, #12; loses to #29, #15, #4, #30 (3–4): "project is
  already dead" kills outright, but only meetings about that project.
- #12 beats #3, #11; loses to the rest (2–5): a cancellation notice, not a
  replacement — criterion 1 favours lines that carry the meeting's content.
- #3 beats #11; loses to the rest (1–6): commentary on the meeting, not a
  replacement of it.
- #11 loses all (0–7): clever meta, but the entry is not an invite, so it does
  not stand alone cleanly.

## Winner

**#29 (25/25)** — 122 chars / 122 bytes, one sentence, no trailing newline:

> The entire meeting fits in this sentence: the launch is delayed a week, the client knows, and nobody here can change that.

Why it wins duels: the line literally performs the task — it IS the whole
meeting, in one sentence. It carries three facts a real meeting would exist to
communicate (the delay, the client already knows, nothing here can change it),
so after it no one can justify holding the meeting (criterion 2, strongest
possible). A delayed launch is the most universal office news there is
(criterion 3). The turn is the opening clause itself — "the entire meeting fits
in this sentence" — a deadpan boast the rest of the line then proves true
(criterion 4). It needs no context: the meeting, the launch, the client and the
room are all inside the sentence (criterion 5). At 122 bytes it is also small,
which the judge's own tie-break favours. Runner-up #15 (no-update status
meeting, 24/25) is the resubmit variant if the parent re-enters. Third #4
(already done in four minutes, 24/25).

Deliberately avoided: "This meeting could have been an email" / "per my last
email" (the most duplicated lines in a 1,275-entry field, and the most likely
house anchor texts — reproducing one takes no prize); the two visible
competitor titles ("We are all here because nobody was brave enough to
reply-all 'no'" and "I already emailed everyone the answer; you're just here
to watch me read it out loud") — #8 and #20 were scored down for closeness.
